# NCP NDA Review Handoff
Oct 2, 2026 · @Trey
## Purpose and how to use this handoff
This document collects every standing rule New Capital Partners (NCP) applies when reviewing, negotiating and redlining NDAs. Anyone picking up an NCP NDA should be able to run a full review from this page alone.
NCP is usually the buyer and the Recipient of confidential information on M&A deals. The default deliverable is a redlined Word document with tracked changes, authored as "New Capital Partners."
How to work through a new NDA, in order:
Confirm NCP's role (Recipient, Disclosing Party, or mutual) before touching any clause. See Section 3.
Confirm whether the target could compete with any NCP business or portfolio company. If unknown, ask Trey before finalizing.
Check the form against the non-negotiables (Section 4) and the standard terms (Section 5).
Decide what to push, what to trade and what to accept using the concession framework (Section 6).
Escalate anything on the counsel red-flag list (Section 7).
Build the redline using the locked workflow and technical pipeline (Sections 8 and 9).
Report back to Trey, flagging every item accepted without his explicit instruction.
Nothing here is legal advice. Outside counsel reviews final language where flagged.
## Signing details and NCP party information
Always fill these in. Never leave NCP's party details as placeholders or blanks.
Why the management company signs: the affiliate, Representative and imputed-knowledge provisions all key off the named Party. Naming a fund vehicle instead can change who is bound and whose knowledge counts.
## Intake gate to run before any redline
Two questions must be answered before any edit is applied. Getting either wrong produces a redline that argues against NCP's own interest.
### Gate 1: What is NCP's role?
Confirm whether NCP is the Recipient, the Disclosing Party, or both under a mutual form. This is the most common error pattern on past reviews.
As Recipient, NCP wants the confidentiality carve-outs broad. Never narrow them.
Disclosing Party moves (narrowing carve-outs, tightening definitions) must not be applied when NCP is receiving.
Lenders go in NCP's own Representatives definition. Do not add them to a shared definition in a way that benefits the other side.
On a mutual form, identify who is the net discloser. A shorter term or narrower obligation can favor NCP even when it looks like a loss.
### Gate 2: Could the target compete with NCP?
If Trey has not said whether the target or counterparty could compete with any NCP business or portfolio company, ask him before finalizing the redline.
If competitive overlap exists, apply the full protection set:
Portfolio company non-imputation (portfolio companies are not deemed to hold NCP's knowledge).
Non-solicit carve-out for portfolio companies.
Dual Hat Employees clause (Section 5).
Hardened Competing Investments clause (Section 4).
If there is no overlap, apply the standard protection set only. The Competing Investments clause is still required on every deal.
### Gate 3: Read the sell-side pattern
Note whether the form comes from a banker or agent on behalf of an unnamed company, or directly from the company. Banker forms tend to carry standstills, broad no-contact lists and tail provisions. Company-direct forms tend to be simpler but may lack PE protections entirely.
## Non-negotiables
These five items are never waived. If a counterparty strikes one, it goes back in, and Trey is told.
Standalone Competing Investments / Nature of Business clause. It must be its own clause, not a proviso buried elsewhere. It affirmatively states that:
NCP is in the business of evaluating, making and managing investments across many companies and industries.
NCP and its Affiliates may evaluate, pursue and make competing or portfolio investments, including in competitors of the Disclosing Party.
Nothing in the NDA limits those activities, provided NCP does not use the Disclosing Party's confidential information in breach.
A residuals concept is included as a backup.
Competing Investments survival and supremacy (the Black Rose rule). The clause survives termination indefinitely and supersedes any contrary current or future agreement. This applies on every deal.
No non-compete language, ever. Watch for non-solicits or no-contact provisions drafted broadly enough to work as a non-compete.
Lenders in the Representatives definition. Lenders and financing sources must be permitted recipients.
PE language required. NCP's right to pursue competitive investments must be protected in the document.
The only recorded exception to items 1 and 2 is the Copa outbound, where indefinite survival and supremacy were traded away. That was a Copa-only trade and is not a precedent for any other deal (see Section 10).
## Standard terms NCP accepts or proposes
These are NCP's opening positions on every form. Mark up any clause that falls short of them.
### Black Rose additions (include on every deal)
Dual Hat Employees clause. Portfolio companies are not deemed to receive confidential information, or to be restricted, solely because NCP personnel sit on their boards. The condition is that the dual-hat person does not pass confidential information to portfolio company personnel.
Click-through and data room supremacy. The NDA governs over any current or future data room or click-through terms.
Legal-compulsion refinements.
Any cooperation is at the Disclosing Party's sole cost.
Recipient's counsel decides the scope of required disclosure. Internal counsel is acceptable.
Regulatory and bank-examiner requests are carved out with no notice required.
Competing Investments hardening. Indefinite survival plus supremacy over contrary current or future agreements (repeats non-negotiable 2).
### Other provisions NCP has added on prior deals
The Earthling Security markup added two clauses worth reusing when a form lacks them:
No restriction on other investments; no imputed knowledge. Knowledge is limited to individuals who actually receive information, and portfolio companies are not Affiliates or Representatives unless they actually receive confidential information.
No obligation to proceed. Neither side must negotiate or close, either may walk away without liability, and nothing binds until a definitive written agreement is signed.

## Counsel red flags and escalation
Four provisions always go to outside counsel. Do not negotiate them alone; flag them to Trey and recommend counsel review.
Liquidated damages.
Indemnification clauses.
Exclusivity provisions.
Any provision binding NCP's LPs.
Outside counsel Trey routes drafting to includes Brad Wood and Carter Dukes. Counsel's toolchain is Litera Compare and iManage, with Ontra producing interim drafts that counsel refines.
### Traps to watch for on every review
These are not automatic escalations, but each needs a deliberate call:
Standstill provisions, including ones that are buried or labeled as something else.
Overly broad definitions of confidential information.
Non-solicitation drafted so broadly it works as a non-compete.
Residuals clauses (check whether the language helps or hurts NCP in the role it holds).
Sell-side banker forms versus company-direct forms.
Forum selection that moves disputes away from Delaware.
Tail provisions that extend obligations past the stated term.
## Locked redline workflow
This sequence is locked. It produces one clean layer of NCP changes on top of the counterparty's text, and it is the same on the first round and every counter-round.
Accept the counterparty's changes. Open their version and accept every tracked change. The result is their clean baseline.
Write NCP's position as clean text. Build a separate plain document with NCP's edits already applied and no tracked changes.
Generate the redline by comparison. Compare the baseline to NCP's clean text using LibreOffice CompareDocuments. Do not use pandoc or python-docx to create the markup.
Keep it to a single layer. Never stack NCP's tracked changes on top of the counterparty's tracked changes.
Audit in full before delivery. Never skip this step.
List every tracked change.
Accept-audit: the redline with all changes accepted must equal NCP's clean text exactly.
Reject-audit: the redline with all changes rejected must equal the counterparty's baseline exactly.
PDF spot-check of the rendered pages.
Confirm there are no orphaned XML elements.
Deliver the redlined .docx (and a clean copy where useful) to /mnt/user-data/outputs/.
The accept-audit and reject-audit are the authoritative tests of correctness. The redline as it looks on screen is not.
### Before you start a counter-round: check the incoming file
Counterparties sometimes send a "redline" with the changes already accepted. On Earthling Security, both incoming .docx files had zero tracked changes and identical text. Before trusting an incoming markup:
Count the w:ins and w:del elements in the file's XML.
If both counts are zero, the file has no live markup. Diff it against the all-changes-accepted version of NCP's last turn to find what they changed.
Ask the counterparty to send future turns with Track Changes on.
## DOCX technical pipeline, silent-failure traps, and mandatory audits
The biggest risk is a silent failure: a script that runs without error but quietly skips an edit. Every step below exists to catch one.
A .docx file is a zip folder of XML files. The pipeline unzips it, edits the XML text, zips it back, and lets LibreOffice compare two versions to produce tracked changes.
### Step-by-step build sequence
Unzip the source .docx into a working folder. Then delete any symbolic links inside it with find -type l -delete.
Defragment the text by running merge_runs.py from /mnt/skills/public/docx/scripts/. Word splits words across many tiny XML pieces; if you skip this, text searches fail without any error message.
Clean up leftover artifacts. Remove every <w:lastRenderedPageBreak/> tag and join any adjacent </w:t><w:t> splits.
Apply each edit with a uniqueness check. Before every Python string replacement, assert the old text appears exactly once: assert xml.count(old) == 1. If the assertion fails, stop and fix the match.
Re-zip the folder with zip -Xqr.
Validate the file with /mnt/skills/public/docx/scripts/office/validate.py, passing the --original and --author flags. Run it after every build, against the final output path rather than a working copy.
Generate the tracked changes through LibreOffice CompareDocuments over its UNO socket (uno:socket,host=127.0.0.1,port=2002). Wait 12 seconds for LibreOffice to start and retry the connection up to 20 times.
Clean the compare output by running accept_changes.py and then merge_runs.py on it before making any further edits.
Fix authorship and IDs. Replace every w:author value with "New Capital Partners" and renumber the change IDs into the 960+ range.
Run the accept-audit and reject-audit with pandoc --track-changes=accept and pandoc --track-changes=reject, comparing each result to its target using Python's difflib.
Stage the output in /mnt/user-data/outputs/.

### Close-but-not-quite cases and inconsistencies to resolve
These are places where the records disagree or a deal departed from the rules. Each needs a decision from Trey.
Apollo term request. The ladder covers counter-offers to 2-year and 3 to 5-year proposals. It does not say what to do when a counterparty pushes 1 year up to 18 months. The 18-month non-solicit is inside the fallback; the 18-month term is not explicitly covered.
Earthling departed from several standards. Virginia law instead of Delaware, three years accepted in Round 1, and no standalone Competing Investments clause with survival and supremacy on record. These were Trey's call on that deal, but they should not be read as precedent.
Earthling Round 3 bypassed the locked workflow. Changes were written directly onto Earthling's file to keep their formatting, rather than generated through CompareDocuments. Confirm whether small conforming edits may skip the compare step.
Earthling signing entity. Recorded as an open item, but the standing rule says NCP Management Holdings, LLC signs. That rule likely resolves it.
Liability wording. One record says "direct damages only"; the other adds "by final court order." Section 5 uses both.
Residuals. Required as a backup inside the Competing Investments clause, but also listed as a trap to watch. The likely reading: NCP's own residuals language is wanted, a counterparty's version needs scrutiny.
## Drafting style and communication rules
Trey is fluent in PE deal conventions and works fast. Reports back to him should be short, direct and honest about weak spots.
Flag every silent acceptance. Any item accepted without Trey's explicit instruction gets called out. That includes a counterparty deleting something NCP had inserted. Never act on it quietly.
Flag close-but-not-quite cases. If a request lands just outside a fallback, say so plainly rather than rounding it in.
Self-audit before presenting. Check the redline and the recommendation against these rules before sending.
Separate the buckets. Tell Trey which changes are non-negotiable, which are trades, and which were accepted as drafted.
Explain direction of benefit. When a counterparty change helps NCP (for example a shorter term when NCP is the net receiver), say so, so it is not resisted by reflex.
No em dashes or long dashes in anything drafted for Trey or the counterparty. Use commas, periods or parentheses instead.
Cover emails. Keep them brief, describe each change in a sentence, and raise process issues (such as a file arriving without live tracked changes).
